The first choice is the one that matters: whether it arrives on a schedule you can count on, or simply turns up.

On a schedule
Salary, rent, a retainer. It accrues month by month, feeds the forecast and the timeline, and shows up as expected income before it lands.
Whenever it comes
Freelance, a bounty, a gift. Nothing is assumed — it counts only once you record it, so it can never flatter the forecast.

Today the app hardcodes two numbers — a $5,000 salary and $500 of car rental — and accrues them at the live rate for every month since the snapshot. Making sources data means that accrual sums whatever is scheduled and still running on each date, so a contract that ends, a raise, or a second job all land in the arithmetic instead of needing the constants edited by hand.

Occasional sources are deliberately left out of the accrual and the forecast. Averaging past freelance work into a projection makes the future look steadier than it is.
